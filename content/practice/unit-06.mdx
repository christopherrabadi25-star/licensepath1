# Unit 6 — Marketing Listings & Advertising Compliance

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this unit you will be able to:

1. **Apply** California's advertising identification rules to every medium you use.
2. **Recognize** and avoid blind ads across signage, social media, and digital campaigns.
3. **Write** listing copy that is compliant, accurate, and effective.
4. **Distinguish** lawful puffing from actionable misrepresentation in marketing.
5. **Apply** fair housing rules to copy, imagery, and audience targeting.
6. **Explain** the rules governing photography, virtual staging, and AI-generated imagery.
7. **Comply** with the rules for advertising sold and pending properties and other agents' listings.

---

## 1. Everything You Publish Is Advertising

New agents think of "advertising" as flyers and signs. The DRE's view is broader: **solicitation materials** include your signs, business cards, flyers, mailers, websites, email, text messages, social media posts, video, and paid ads. An Instagram story about a listing is advertising and is governed accordingly.

Two rules govern nearly everything:

**Rule 1 — Identify yourself.** Solicitation materials must identify the licensee and the responsible broker, and must display the license number as required by regulation. <!-- VERIFY: current advertising identification and license number display requirements, including any exemptions for small items -->

**Rule 2 — No blind ads.** An advertisement that fails to reveal that the advertiser is a **licensee acting as an agent** is a blind ad and is prohibited (Principles Unit 1). The classic violation is a listing post with a photo, a price, and a phone number and nothing else.

The most common real-world violation is not a flyer. It is a personal social media account where an agent posts listings without brokerage identification, because the account "isn't really business."

## 2. Writing the Copy

Listing copy has three jobs at once: attract the right buyer, describe the property accurately, and stay compliant. The good news is that these rarely conflict — compliant copy is usually better copy, because it describes the property rather than the imagined buyer.

**The governing rule from Principles Unit 15: describe the property, never the people.**

**Words that create fair housing exposure:**

| Avoid | Characteristic implicated |
|---|---|
| "Perfect for a young family" | Familial status, age |
| "Ideal for professionals" | Familial status, source of income |
| "Walking distance to St. Anne's" | Religion |
| "Adult community" (unqualified) | Familial status |
| "No Section 8" | Source of income (California) |
| "English-speaking tenants preferred" | National origin, primary language |
| "Safe, family neighborhood" | Familial status, plus a factual claim risk |
| "Great for empty nesters" | Familial status, age |

**Words that create factual-representation exposure**, separate from fair housing:

- **"Safe"** — an unverifiable claim about crime.
- **"Best schools"** — a claim about a district you do not control; use published ratings and attribute them.
- **"Permitted"** — never say it without seeing the permit.
- **Square footage** — cite the source (assessor, appraisal, builder) and note that buyer verification is recommended. Square footage disputes are among the most common misrepresentation claims.
- **"Steps from"** — measure it or omit it.

**Puffing vs. misrepresentation** (Principles Unit 6): "charming," "stunning," and "must-see" are non-factual opinion no reasonable buyer relies on. Anything verifiable — size, zoning, permits, school assignment, HOA dues, year built — is a statement of fact, and if wrong it is a misrepresentation even when innocent.

**Terminology note:** the industry has broadly moved from "master bedroom" to "primary bedroom." Follow your MLS's current convention.

## 3. Photography and Imagery

**Photographs are representations.** A wide-angle lens that makes a 9-foot bedroom look 14 feet is a misrepresentation risk, not a technique.

**Virtual staging** is widely used and lawful — but it must be **disclosed**. Label virtually staged photos clearly. An unlabeled virtually staged image showing furniture in an empty room misleads about condition and scale.

**Digital alteration** beyond staging is where agents get into trouble: removing power lines, editing out a neighbor's structure, replacing a brown lawn with green, or altering a view. Each of those is a misrepresentation of the property's actual condition.

**AI-generated or AI-enhanced imagery** raises the same issue in sharper form. If an image does not depict the property as it actually is, it must be disclosed as an alteration or not used. Treat AI enhancement exactly as you would virtual staging: permitted, labeled, and never used to conceal a condition. <!-- VERIFY: current MLS rules and any California statutory requirements on AI-generated listing imagery -->

**Copyright matters.** Listing photographs are copyrighted works, typically owned by the photographer unless the agreement transfers rights. Using another agent's listing photos — including from a prior listing of the same property — without permission is infringement, and photographers do pursue it. Get a written license with your photographer that covers your intended uses.

**Imagery and fair housing.** Marketing imagery that consistently depicts one demographic can itself signal a preference. This is a real, documented enforcement theory.

## 4. Advertising Other Agents' Listings, Sold Properties, and Yourself

**Other brokers' listings.** MLS rules generally permit displaying other participants' listings through IDX, subject to attribution requirements. Advertising another broker's listing as though it were yours — or without required attribution — violates both MLS rules and the ethical duty toward other licensees.

**Sold and pending properties.** You may advertise a sale you were involved in, but the ad must **not** imply you were the listing agent if you were the buyer's agent, or vice versa. "Just sold — represented the buyer" is accurate; a "SOLD" sign rider on a listing you did not list is not.

**Team and personal branding.** Team names must comply with the statutory rules (Unit 1), and all team advertising must identify the responsible broker.

**Testimonials and results claims.** If you advertise production statistics, they must be accurate and not misleading. "Sold 12 homes last year" must be 12 homes you actually represented, not 12 your team closed. Ambiguity here is a misrepresentation problem.

**Compensation claims.** Because compensation practices have changed materially in recent years, be careful with any blanket statement about who pays what. Verify current requirements before making claims about buyer or seller compensation in advertising. <!-- VERIFY: current rules and MLS policies on advertising compensation -->

## 5. Digital Channels

**Your website** must carry the licensee and broker identification and license number, on every page a consumer might land on — not buried in an "About" page.

**Social media** is advertising. The identification requirements do not relax because the platform is casual. Practical approach: put your license number and brokerage in your profile bio, and include brokerage identification in posts featuring listings.

**Paid digital advertising** raises the fair housing targeting issues covered in Unit 2 — housing ads face platform restrictions specifically because of enforcement over discriminatory targeting. Do not select or exclude audiences by protected characteristics or their proxies.

**Email and text** are governed by CAN-SPAM and the telephone rules (Unit 2). A listing announcement blasted to purchased numbers is the same violation whether or not it concerns a property.

**Review platforms.** Do not write, solicit under false pretenses, or incentivize fake reviews. Beyond the ethical problem, it is a deceptive practice.

## 6. Worked Examples

### Example 1 — Rewriting non-compliant copy (San Gabriel Valley)

**Draft:**

> *"Charming starter home in a quiet, safe family neighborhood. Walking distance to St. Rita's. Perfect for a young couple or first-time buyers. 1,900 sq ft with permitted bonus room. Master suite upstairs. No Section 8. Best schools in the area!"*

**Line-by-line:**

| Phrase | Problem |
|---|---|
| "starter home" | Implies a buyer profile; borderline familial status |
| "safe" | Unverifiable crime claim — misrepresentation risk |
| "family neighborhood" | Familial status |
| "Walking distance to St. Rita's" | Religion |
| "Perfect for a young couple" | Familial status, age, marital status |
| "1,900 sq ft" | Fact — must cite source |
| "permitted bonus room" | Fact — must verify the permit exists |
| "Master suite" | Superseded terminology |
| "No Section 8" | **Source of income discrimination in California** |
| "Best schools" | Unverifiable superlative about a district |

**Rewritten:**

> *"Three-bedroom, two-bath home on a quiet cul-de-sac. Updated kitchen with quartz counters and stainless appliances; primary suite upstairs with walk-in closet. Bonus room off the garage (permit records available — buyer to verify). Approximately 1,900 sq ft per assessor; buyer to verify. Detached two-car garage, covered patio, mature landscaping. Close to parks, transit, and shopping. Equal Housing Opportunity."*

The rewrite is compliant **and better marketing** — it tells a buyer what they are actually purchasing instead of who the writer imagined buying it.

### Example 2 — The Instagram blind ad

An agent posts to her personal Instagram: a listing photo, "$849,000 — 3 bed 2 bath in Covina, DM me!" Her bio says only her name and "Realtor."

**Analysis.** This is likely a **blind ad**. It advertises property for sale without identifying the responsible broker, and the bio does not cure it — the required identification is missing from the material advertising the property. The casual platform changes nothing.

**Fix:** include the brokerage name and license number in the post itself (caption or image overlay), and carry brokerage identification and license number in the profile bio as a baseline. Build the habit now; retrofitting a year of posts is unpleasant.

### Example 3 — Virtual staging gone wrong (Ontario)

An agent virtually stages an empty living room. The photo shows a sofa, rug, and coffee table, and is not labeled. A buyer writes an offer sight-unseen from out of state, then discovers at walk-through that the room is substantially smaller than it appeared and the flooring is damaged — the virtual rug covered it.

**Analysis.** Two problems. The **failure to label** the staging is a misrepresentation of the property's condition. Worse, the virtual furnishing **concealed a defect**, which moves this from a labeling oversight toward active concealment (Principles Unit 9).

**The rule:** label every virtually staged image, and never place virtual elements over a condition a buyer would want to see. If the floor is damaged, the photo shows the damaged floor.

### Example 4 — The square footage claim (Arcadia)

A listing advertises **2,450 sq ft**, taken from a prior listing. The assessor shows **2,180**. A permitted addition accounts for part of the difference; the rest is an enclosed patio that was never permitted. The buyer closes, discovers the discrepancy, and sues.

**Analysis.** Repeating a figure from a prior listing is not verification, and innocent misrepresentation of a material fact can still support rescission or damages (Principles Unit 6). The unpermitted enclosure is separately material and should have been disclosed.

**The practice:** cite your source ("approximately 2,180 sq ft per assessor"), note buyer verification, and where square footage is genuinely uncertain, say so and recommend an appraisal or measurement. Square footage claims are among the most common misrepresentation claims in residential practice, and the fix costs one sentence.

## 7. Key Terms

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Solicitation materials** | All advertising by a licensee: signs, print, web, email, text, social media, and video. |
| **Blind ad** | An advertisement failing to reveal that the advertiser is a licensee acting as an agent; prohibited. |
| **License identification requirement** | The obligation to display the licensee's name, license number, and responsible broker as required by regulation. |
| **Puffing** | Non-factual exaggerated opinion no reasonable buyer relies on; distinguished from factual claims. |
| **Virtual staging** | Digitally added furnishings in listing photos; lawful but must be disclosed and must never conceal a condition. |
| **IDX** | Internet Data Exchange — the MLS framework permitting display of other participants' listings with attribution. |
| **Equal Housing Opportunity** | The fair housing logo and statement required in advertising. |
| **Attribution** | Required credit to the listing broker when displaying another broker's listing. |

## 8. Practice Tips

- **Every post is advertising.** Put brokerage identification and your license number where a consumer will see them.
- **Describe the property, never the people.** Run every draft against that single test.
- **Cite the source for every fact** — square footage, permits, HOA dues, year built — and recommend buyer verification.
- **Label virtual staging**, and never let a virtual element cover a defect.
- **Never say "safe," "best schools," or "permitted"** without verified support.
- **"No Section 8" is a violation in California.** It should never appear in your copy.
- **Get a written photo license** from your photographer covering your intended uses.
- **Do not advertise a sale in a way that implies a role you did not have.**
