# Unit 6 — Escrow & Closing Law; RESPA in Depth

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this unit you will be able to:

1. **Explain** the legal nature of escrow as a dual agency of limited purpose, distinct from real estate agency.
2. **Identify** who may lawfully act as an escrow holder in California and why that matters.
3. **Apply** the "meeting of the minds" requirement for a valid escrow to exist.
4. **Explain** RESPA's core prohibitions and how they apply to real estate referral relationships.
5. **Analyze** a real fact pattern for a Section 8 kickback violation.
6. **Distinguish** RESPA's federal requirements from California's separate escrow and closing rules.
7. **Advise** (within license scope) on how to keep referral and disclosure practices compliant.

## 1. Escrow as a Legal Relationship

Escrow is not merely an office that holds money — it is a specific legal relationship: a **dual agency of limited purpose**, in which the escrow holder acts as the agent of *both* buyer and seller (and, for loan-related instructions, the lender), but only within the scope of the escrow instructions both parties have signed.

This is fundamentally different from real estate brokerage agency. A real estate agent has broad fiduciary duties — loyalty, advice, negotiation. An escrow holder has a narrow, contractual duty: to follow the mutual written instructions exactly, hold funds and documents neutrally, and take no side. An escrow holder **cannot give legal or real estate advice to either party** and must remain strictly neutral between them.

**A valid escrow requires:**

1. A **binding contract** between the parties (the purchase agreement).
2. **Conditional delivery** of transfer instruments (typically the deed) to a third party.
3. Instructions specifying what must happen before the escrow holder releases documents and funds.

Escrow does not create the contract — it *executes* one that already exists. This is why escrow instructions must be consistent with the underlying purchase agreement, not a substitute for it; a conflict between the escrow instructions and the purchase contract is a real problem, not a technicality, and needs to be resolved and documented, not glossed over.

## 2. Who May Lawfully Conduct Escrow in California

California regulates who may act as an escrow holder. Independent escrow companies must generally be licensed by the **Department of Financial Protection and Innovation (DFPI)** as escrow agents, subject to bonding, trust account, and reporting requirements. <!-- VERIFY: current DFPI escrow licensing statute and exemption list --> Certain parties are exempted from separate escrow licensing when acting within their own regulated capacity — for example, banks, attorneys handling an escrow incidental to their practice, and title companies handling escrow incidental to issuing title insurance are treated differently than a stand-alone escrow company. <!-- VERIFY: exact statutory exemptions and their current scope -->

**Why this matters to a licensee:** a real estate broker generally may not simply "hold" a transaction's funds informally, outside a licensed escrow or the broker's own regulated trust account process (Principles Unit 18). Steering clients toward an unlicensed, uninsured entity to "save time" is a real liability exposure, not a convenience.

## 3. RESPA: Federal Law Governing Settlement Services

The **Real Estate Settlement Procedures Act (RESPA)** is a federal law regulating the process around residential mortgage loan settlement services. Its core purposes: require disclosure of settlement costs to consumers, and **prohibit kickbacks and unearned fees** that inflate the cost of getting a loan without providing genuine value.

### Section 8: The Anti-Kickback Rule

**RESPA Section 8** prohibits giving or accepting **any fee, kickback, or thing of value** in exchange for the referral of settlement service business involving a federally related mortgage loan. It also prohibits giving or accepting **unearned fees** — charging for a settlement service and splitting that charge with someone who did no work to earn a share of it.

**What this reaches, concretely:**

- A lender paying an agent a fee for every buyer referred to that lender.
- A title company giving an agent free marketing services in exchange for referring escrow business.
- A home warranty company paying an agent's marketing costs in exchange for referrals, disguised as a "marketing services agreement" with no genuine services actually performed.
- A broker requiring buyers to use an affiliated title or escrow company **without disclosing the affiliation and without the buyer having a genuine choice.**

**What RESPA does allow:** payment for services actually rendered at a fair market rate, and disclosed **Affiliated Business Arrangements (AfBAs)** — where a broker has an ownership interest in a title, escrow, or mortgage company and refers clients to it, provided the affiliation is disclosed in writing, the client is not required to use the affiliated entity, and the fees charged are not above what is generally available in the marketplace.

### Worked Example 1 — The disguised marketing agreement (Inland Empire)

A title company offers to pay an agent's team $2,000 a month for "marketing services," structured as a flat retainer with no specific deliverables tracked, in exchange for the team's escrow referrals.

**Analysis.** If the $2,000 payment does not correspond to actual, fairly valued marketing work performed, this is a **disguised kickback for referrals** — the "marketing services agreement" label does not change the substance. RESPA looks at what actually happened, not what the contract is titled. An agent offered an arrangement like this should treat it as a serious compliance red flag and involve the broker before agreeing to anything, not after signing.

### Worked Example 2 — The properly disclosed affiliated business (Ontario)

A brokerage has a minority ownership stake in an escrow company. When referring a buyer to that escrow company, the agent provides a written **Affiliated Business Arrangement disclosure** stating the ownership interest, states the buyer is not required to use the affiliated company, and confirms the escrow fee is consistent with market rates.

**Analysis.** This is the compliant version of essentially the same underlying business relationship as Example 1 — the difference is disclosure, genuine choice, and fair pricing, all done in writing and in advance. The structure itself (an affiliated business) is not the violation; concealment and forced use are.

## 4. RESPA's Disclosure Requirements

Beyond Section 8, RESPA (as implemented and integrated with TILA disclosure requirements) governs the timing and content of loan-related disclosures the buyer receives from their lender — informing the buyer of estimated and actual settlement costs. <!-- VERIFY: current integrated disclosure form names and timing requirements, which changed materially after the TILA-RESPA Integrated Disclosure rule --> Real estate agents do not prepare these lender-side disclosures, but understanding that they exist — and roughly when the buyer should receive them — helps an agent set accurate expectations and recognize if a lender is moving unusually slowly.

## 5. California Escrow and Closing Rules Beyond RESPA

RESPA is federal and focused specifically on settlement-service referrals and kickbacks tied to federally related mortgage loans. California layers its own separate rules on top:

- **Escrow licensing and trust accounting** (DFPI regulation, discussed above) applies regardless of whether a loan is involved.
- **Good funds requirements** — California law restricts when escrow may disburse funds based on the type of instrument used to fund the transaction (e.g., wire transfer versus a personal check), to reduce the risk of disbursing against funds that later fail to clear. <!-- VERIFY: current California good funds statute thresholds and covered instrument types -->
- **Natural Hazard Disclosure and other state-mandated disclosures** flow through escrow but are governed by separate California statutes, not RESPA (Principles Unit 9).
- **Cancellation and cooperation obligations** — escrow's neutral role does not exempt it from following properly executed mutual cancellation instructions promptly once received.

The practical takeaway: RESPA and California escrow law overlap in the closing process but are not the same body of law, and a transaction can be fully RESPA-compliant while still violating a California-specific escrow or disclosure rule, or vice versa.

## 6. Wire Fraud: The Closing-Stage Risk Escrow Law Does Not Fully Solve

A closing-stage risk worth naming even though it is not itself a RESPA or licensing issue: **business email compromise / wire fraud**, where a criminal intercepts email communications near closing and sends the buyer fraudulent wiring instructions that appear to come from escrow. Buyers who wire funds to the fraudulent account typically cannot recover them.

**Professional practice, every transaction:** never send wiring instructions by email without a verbal confirmation using a phone number independently verified (not a number provided in the same email), and warn buyers about this risk in writing, early, before they are anywhere near the closing table. This is not escrow's legal duty alone to prevent — it is a shared professional responsibility, and an agent who fails to warn a client about this well-known risk is exposing both the client and their own liability.

## 7. Key Terms

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Escrow** | A dual agency of limited purpose that executes mutual written instructions neutrally. |
| **DFPI (Department of Financial Protection and Innovation)** | California regulator overseeing licensed independent escrow companies. |
| **RESPA** | The federal Real Estate Settlement Procedures Act governing settlement-service disclosures and prohibiting kickbacks. |
| **Section 8 (RESPA)** | The anti-kickback provision prohibiting fees for referrals and splitting unearned fees. |
| **Affiliated Business Arrangement (AfBA)** | A disclosed ownership relationship between a referring party and a settlement-service provider, permitted with proper disclosure, choice, and fair pricing. |
| **Good funds requirement** | California rules restricting escrow disbursement based on the type and clearance of the funding instrument. |
| **Wire fraud (business email compromise)** | Fraudulent interception of closing communications to redirect a buyer's wired funds. |

## 8. Exam and Practice Tips

- Escrow is a dual agency of *limited purpose* — it follows instructions, it does not give advice or take sides.
- RESPA Section 8 prohibits both kickbacks for referrals and splitting unearned fees — know both halves.
- A "marketing services agreement" that pays for referrals without real services performed is a disguised kickback regardless of its label.
- Affiliated Business Arrangements are lawful with disclosure, genuine choice, and market-rate pricing.
- RESPA is federal and loan-focused; California layers separate escrow licensing, trust accounting, and disclosure rules on top.
- Warn every buyer about wire fraud risk in writing, early — before closing week.
