# Unit 2 — Prospecting & Lead Generation

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this unit you will be able to:

1. **Build** and segment a sphere of influence database and explain why it outperforms cold sources.
2. **Design** a geographic farm using absorption rate and turnover data.
3. **Apply** the legal rules governing telephone, text, and email solicitation.
4. **Distinguish** lawful FSBO and expired-listing outreach from prohibited practices.
5. **Explain** referral fee rules and who may lawfully be paid one.
6. **Calculate** cost per lead, conversion rates, and return on marketing spend.
7. **Identify** fair housing risks embedded in targeting and audience selection.

---

## 1. Where Business Actually Comes From

New agents overwhelmingly want a lead source they can buy. Experienced agents will tell you the same thing every time: the highest-converting business comes from people who already know you.

The rough hierarchy, by conversion rate rather than volume:

1. **Repeat clients and direct referrals** — highest conversion, lowest cost, longest lag.
2. **Sphere of influence** — everyone you know. High conversion, requires consistency.
3. **Geographic farm** — a defined neighborhood you own the mindshare of. Slow to build, durable once built.
4. **Past-client database** — reactivation over years, not months.
5. **Open houses and community presence** — moderate conversion, immediate.
6. **Online portal leads and paid ads** — high volume, low conversion, immediate cost.
7. **Cold outreach: FSBOs, expireds, circle prospecting** — low conversion, high rejection, heavily regulated.

New agents typically start at the bottom of that list because it feels less personally exposing than calling people they know. It is also where the worst economics are. The uncomfortable truth is that telling forty people you know that you got licensed will outperform a month of portal leads.

## 2. Sphere of Influence

Your **sphere of influence (SOI)** is everyone you have a relationship with: family, friends, former colleagues, classmates, neighbors, the parents at your kid's school, your barber, your church or mosque or temple community, your old restaurant coworkers. For most new agents this is 150 to 400 people, and it is worth more than any lead source you can buy.

**Build it properly:**

1. **Export everything** — phone contacts, email contacts, social connections. Do not curate yet.
2. **Put it in a CRM**, not a spreadsheet you will abandon.
3. **Segment it**: A (would refer you without being asked), B (knows you, would respond), C (weak connection).
4. **Announce once, clearly.** Tell people you are licensed, what you do, and how to refer you. Then stop announcing and start being useful.
5. **Touch consistently.** A quarterly rhythm of genuinely useful contact — market updates specific to their neighborhood, a note when a comparable sells near them — beats monthly generic newsletters that get deleted.

The discipline that matters is **consistency, not cleverness**. An agent who contacts 200 people four times a year, every year, will out-earn one who runs brilliant campaigns for three months and stops.

## 3. Geographic Farming

A **farm** is a defined geographic area you commit to owning. Farming is a multi-year investment: expect 6 to 18 months before meaningful return.

**Choosing a farm — the numbers:**

- **Turnover rate** = homes sold in the last 12 months ÷ total homes in the area. Below roughly 4–5% is slow going.
- **Absorption rate** = how quickly current inventory would sell at the current pace. It tells you whether the market favors sellers or buyers.
- **Competition** — if one agent already holds 40% of the listings, pick a different farm or accept a long fight.
- **Size** — pick something you can genuinely touch consistently. 300 to 500 homes is a common starting range; 2,000 homes you contact once is worthless.

**Farm math:** if a 400-home farm turns over at 5%, that is **20 sales per year**. Capturing 15% of them is **3 transactions** — meaningful, and realistic only after sustained presence.

**What actually works in a farm:** consistent mail with genuinely useful data (not just "just sold" cards), neighborhood-specific market reports, presence at community events, and being the person whose name residents recognize when they finally decide to sell.

## 4. The Legal Rules of Outreach

This is where new agents create real liability, and the rules are not optional.

### Telephone and text

Federal telemarketing law and the **National Do Not Call Registry** govern calls to consumers. Core points for your practice:

- You must **scrub numbers against the DNC registry** before cold calling, and maintain your own internal do-not-call list.
- An **established business relationship** and **inquiry/permission** create limited exceptions with time limits. <!-- VERIFY: current EBR and inquiry exception durations -->
- **Calling hours** are restricted (commonly 8 a.m. to 9 p.m. in the called party's time zone). <!-- VERIFY: current permitted hours -->
- **Autodialers and prerecorded messages** to cell phones require prior express consent, and this is the area generating the largest damages awards. Do not use them without counsel.
- **Text messages** are treated as calls for these purposes. A text blast to purchased numbers is not a marketing tactic; it is a lawsuit.

Penalties are **per call or text**, which is how a "small" campaign becomes a catastrophic claim.

### Email

The federal **CAN-SPAM** rules require commercial email to include a valid physical postal address, honest subject lines and headers, identification as an advertisement where applicable, and a **working opt-out** honored promptly. <!-- VERIFY: current opt-out honoring deadline -->

### FSBOs and expired listings

Both are **lawful to contact** — subject to the DNC rules above. Neither is a loophole.

The professional approach to a **FSBO** is not to argue that they cannot sell it themselves. It is to offer something useful (a comparable sales analysis, a disclosure checklist, an offer to bring a buyer) and to be the first call when they decide to list. Many FSBOs do eventually list.

For **expired listings**, the seller has just had a disappointing experience and will be contacted by many agents at once. Differentiate by diagnosing honestly — usually price, condition, or exposure — rather than criticizing the prior agent, which reads as unprofessional and violates the ethical duty to avoid disparaging other licensees.

### Referral fees — who may be paid

This is a licensing rule that new agents break casually.

- A broker may pay a **referral fee to another licensed broker**, including out-of-state brokers.
- A salesperson may be paid **only by their employing broker** (Principles Unit 1).
- **Paying an unlicensed person** for referring real estate business is generally prohibited, and it is a violation by the payer. A gift card to a friend who sent you a client is exactly the transaction the rule targets. <!-- VERIFY: current rules and any limited exceptions for nominal gifts to past clients -->
- **RESPA** separately prohibits paying for referrals of settlement services — lenders, title, escrow, home warranty (Principles Unit 12). Calling it a marketing fee changes nothing.

When in doubt: route it through your broker and ask before you pay anyone anything.

## 5. Fair Housing in Targeting

Prospecting is where fair housing violations hide, because targeting tools make discrimination easy to do accidentally.

- **Ad audience selection** based on protected characteristics — or proxies for them — is unlawful. Digital advertising platforms have implemented restrictions on housing ads specifically because of enforcement actions over discriminatory targeting. <!-- VERIFY: current platform housing-ad restrictions -->
- **Geographic targeting that excludes** particular neighborhoods can produce a discriminatory pattern even without discriminatory intent.
- **Ad copy** follows the Principles Unit 15 rule exactly: describe the property, never the people.
- **Farm selection itself** deserves a moment of honesty — choosing farms in a way that systematically avoids certain communities is a pattern worth examining in your own practice.

The protective habit from Principles Unit 15 applies here too: **standardize and audit.** Same response time, same follow-up sequence, same materials for everyone, and look at your own data periodically.

## 6. Measuring What You Spend

Marketing without measurement is how new agents lose money slowly.

**Cost per lead (CPL)** = total spend ÷ leads generated.
**Cost per closing** = total spend ÷ closings from that source.
**Return on investment** = (commission earned − cost) ÷ cost.

Track *by source*. Most agents discover that one or two sources produce nearly all their closings while spend is spread evenly across five.

Also track the **lag**. Portal leads may close in 30 days; farm mail may take 14 months. A source is not failing merely because it has not produced yet — but you need to know which clock you are on.

## 7. Worked Examples

### Example 1 — Farm selection math (Covina vs. Claremont)

Two candidate farms:

| | Farm A (Covina) | Farm B (Claremont) |
|---|---|---|
| Homes | 420 | 380 |
| Sales last 12 months | 27 | 12 |
| Dominant agent's share | 12% | 38% |

**Walkthrough.**
1. Turnover A: 27 ÷ 420 = **6.4%**
2. Turnover B: 12 ÷ 380 = **3.2%**
3. Farm A has more than double the turnover *and* a fragmented competitive field.
4. Realistic capture in A at 15% after two years: 27 × 0.15 = **4 transactions/year**
5. Same capture in B: 12 × 0.15 = **1.8 transactions/year**, against an entrenched competitor holding 38%.

**Farm A.** The lesson is that farm selection is arithmetic before it is preference — many agents farm where they live rather than where the numbers work.

### Example 2 — Return on marketing spend (Inland Empire)

Two sources over 12 months:

**Portal leads:** $9,600 spent, 240 leads, 3 closings. Average commission to you: $8,900.
**Farm mail:** $4,200 spent (400 homes × 6 mailings × ~$1.75), 18 direct inquiries, 2 closings.

**Walkthrough.**
1. Portal CPL: $9,600 ÷ 240 = **$40/lead**; cost per closing: $9,600 ÷ 3 = **$3,200**
2. Portal ROI: (3 × $8,900 − $9,600) ÷ $9,600 = ($26,700 − $9,600) ÷ $9,600 = **178%**
3. Farm CPL: $4,200 ÷ 18 = **$233/lead**; cost per closing: $4,200 ÷ 2 = **$2,100**
4. Farm ROI: (2 × $8,900 − $4,200) ÷ $4,200 = ($17,800 − $4,200) ÷ $4,200 = **324%**

The farm produced fewer leads at a much higher cost per lead — and a **substantially better return**, because farm leads convert far better than portal leads. Judging sources on cost per lead alone would have led you to exactly the wrong conclusion.

### Example 3 — The referral fee you cannot pay (San Gabriel Valley)

Your unlicensed cousin sends you a buyer who closes at $720,000, earning you $9,000. He asks for "a few hundred for the referral."

**Analysis.** You cannot pay him. Compensating an **unlicensed person** for referring real estate business is prohibited, and the violation belongs to **you** as the payer. It does not matter that the amount is small, that he is family, or that you call it a gift for a referral.

What you *can* do: thank him sincerely, take him to dinner as a family member rather than as compensation for business, and be scrupulous about the distinction. If he wants to earn referral income, he needs a license.

Related trap: a **lender** offering you $500 per closed referral is a RESPA violation (Principles Unit 12), regardless of what it is labeled.

### Example 4 — The text campaign that becomes a lawsuit

An agent buys a list of 5,000 phone numbers for homeowners in three ZIP codes and sends a mass text: "Thinking of selling? I have buyers in your neighborhood."

**Analysis.** Multiple exposures at once. The numbers were not scrubbed against the DNC registry, there is no established business relationship or prior express consent, and mass texting to cell numbers implicates the autodialer rules that carry **per-message** statutory damages. At 5,000 messages, even a modest per-message figure produces a catastrophic number.

There is no version of this campaign that is worth running. Build the sphere instead.

## 8. Key Terms

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Sphere of influence (SOI)** | Everyone the agent already has a relationship with; the highest-converting source of business. |
| **Geographic farm** | A defined neighborhood an agent commits to for consistent, long-term marketing presence. |
| **Turnover rate** | Homes sold in twelve months divided by total homes; the primary test of a farm's viability. |
| **Absorption rate** | The pace at which current inventory would sell at the current rate of sales. |
| **Do Not Call Registry** | The federal registry that must be scrubbed before cold calling consumers, with limited exceptions. |
| **Established business relationship (EBR)** | A limited, time-bound exception permitting calls to prior clients and inquirers. |
| **CAN-SPAM** | Federal rules for commercial email: valid postal address, honest headers, working opt-out. |
| **FSBO** | For sale by owner — lawful to contact subject to DNC rules. |
| **Expired listing** | A listing that ended without a sale; lawful to contact, subject to the same rules. |
| **Referral fee** | Compensation for referring business; payable broker-to-broker, never to unlicensed persons. |
| **Cost per lead (CPL)** | Marketing spend divided by leads generated from that source. |
| **Cost per closing** | Marketing spend divided by closings attributable to that source — the more meaningful measure. |

## 9. Practice Tips

- **Build the CRM in week one** and put every contact in it before you need them.
- **Scrub before you call.** DNC compliance is not optional, and penalties are per call.
- **Never mass text purchased numbers.** This is the single fastest way to create a serious claim.
- **Judge sources by cost per closing and ROI**, not cost per lead.
- **Choose farms by turnover and competition**, not by where you happen to live.
- **Route every referral payment question through your broker** before money moves.
- **Audit your own targeting** for patterns you did not intend.
